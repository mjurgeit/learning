## 1. Niches chosen and why
1. **Midlife / perimenopause guided journal** (journal with check-ins and prompts). Demand evidence [blog-claim]: search results show multiple competing titles (Kate Codrington "The Perimenopause Journal", Aimee Benbow "The Menopause Journal" 90-day, Karen Jackson "Menopause Journal" 111pp, Caroline Reed "The Perimenopause Journal" 90-day tracker), and guided-journal categories like gratitude/anxiety/shadow work have bestsellers with 20,000+ ratings ("Good Days Start with Gratitude") [blog-claim]. Competition: moderate and growing; the generic gratitude journal is saturated, the perimenopause sub-niche is newer. Angle: 90 daily pages with a one-minute numeric check-in (sleep, energy, mood, hot flushes), 13 weekly themes and weekly review, non-clinical tone, easy to bring to an appointment. Differentiator vs. competitors: structure + pattern-spotting weekly reviews rather than vague prompts.
2. **Themed large-print word search** (gardening first). Demand evidence [blog-claim]: multiple sources state generic word search is saturated but themed ones (gardening, nursing, Bible, nostalgia, state-specific) still rank because "the theme is the keyword", and large print for seniors is a big, focused market. Price band for large-print seniors word search is about $7.99-$12.99 per search results [blog-claim]. Competition: very high for generic, medium for gardening-themed. Angle: unique-per-puzzle word lists, forward-only directions (easier for seniors), 15x15 grid with 20pt letters, full-page one puzzle, shaded solutions.
3. **Later (not yet built):** large-print sudoku, nostalgia trivia/puzzles for seniors and reminiscence (existing "Trivia for Seniors", "Searching for Nostalgia", "Puzzles for Adults: The Swinging '60s" titles exist [blog-claim]), a health/blood pressure logbook.

Why medium content rather than blank low-content: Amazon increasingly removes low-quality/near-duplicate low-content titles; multiple sources say medium-content books (puzzles, guided journals, workbooks) are treated like regular books and are the more durable direction [blog-claim: Naomi Jane substack, Vappingo, lowcontentprofits].

## 2. Personas
- **"Dana", 49, perimenopausal,** sleeps badly, feels foggy, wants to track patterns and bring notes to the doctor; buys on Amazon, also buys it as a gift for a friend. Problem: vague symptoms, no structure for tracking or reflecting.
- **"Margaret", 72, retired gardener,** reads large print, does puzzles daily; or her daughter buying a gift. Problem: puzzle books with tiny print, repeated puzzles, answer errors.
- **Gift buyers** (adult children, friends) searching "gift for gardener / grandma / her".

- W2: build covers (Canva free/KDP Cover Creator) for books 1-2; fill metadata; order proofs (about $3-6 each + shipping). Check print quality, margins, gutter, readability of grid.

## 9. Traffic plan
Amazon is the traffic. 1) Keyword-rich title/subtitle and 7 boxes. 2) Category fit. 3) Look-inside quality (front matter should show real puzzles: put a sample puzzle as page 5). 4) Reviews from genuine buyers; add "Please leave a review" line on the last page (allowed wording: ask, don't incentivize). 5) KDP Ads, small budgets. 6) Pinterest pins for gift-type searches (gardening gift, menopause gift) linking to the Amazon page (use only on Pinterest, not inside the book). 7) Facebook gardening/caregiver groups. 8) Free printable sample PDF with a mailing-list sign-up (needs a separate Payhip/Gumroad page: handled by the other business folders).

## 10. Risks, rules, kill criteria
- **AI disclosure** [blog-claim, multiple sources]: KDP requires telling it, at publishing, if content (text, images or translations) is AI-generated; "AI-assisted" (brainstorming, editing, grammar help) need not be disclosed. Text written by Claude and lightly edited = AI-generated: disclose. Failing to disclose is the fastest-growing suspension trigger [blog-claim]. The grids are produced by our own script; the word lists and prompts were written by Claude, so disclose AI-generated text. Cover art generated by AI: disclose too.
- **Daily limit:** KDP limits to 3 new titles per day (2023) [verified by news in search results: Authors Guild, ebookfriendly]. Irrelevant at our pace.
- **Duplicate / low quality** [blog-claim]: KDP removes near-duplicate and low-quality titles; reusing the same puzzle interior with a different cover is dangerous. Rule: each book is unique content; never publish the same interior twice; no public-domain-only content; check solutions are correct (our script verifies each word is present).
- **Medical claims:** the journal must not claim to treat or diagnose menopause; disclaimer is on copyright page and description. Do not use "cure", "relieve", "hormone balance". Do not reproduce clinical questionnaires that are copyrighted.
- **IP:** no trademarks (Brain Games, Sudoku brand-specific), no song/film titles in puzzle lists beyond plain facts, no stock art without license, no AI art that copies a style, fonts: Liberation (open license) embedded.
- **Account risk:** one account only; terminated accounts are very hard to recover [blog-claim].
- **Market risk:** saturation; expect most titles to sell near zero; revenue is a portfolio game.
- **Kill criteria:** after 90 days, if total units across the first 5 books are under 15, stop making new books and either fix covers/keywords once, or stop. After 6 months, if monthly royalty is under $50 with 10 books, stop that niche. Stop any ad campaign with ACOS over 80% after 100 clicks. Stop immediately and re-read the guidelines on any KDP warning.
