## Compliance constraint that applies to ALL of these (read first)
- HIPAA covers the buyer's data, not our template. A blank, fillable template, policy kit or tracker contains no PHI, so selling it is fine. Risk starts if we (a) host or process client data (web app, AI add-on, shared Google Sheet), which would make us a business associate needing BAAs, or (b) market a kit as "HIPAA compliant" / "guarantees compliance" (false-assurance and unauthorized-legal-advice claims). [estimate/judgment, not legal advice; have a lawyer review wording.]
- Safe design: offline fillable PDF/Word/Excel, no data leaves the buyer's device, prominent disclaimer "template, not legal advice, adapt to your state." An AI add-on that ingests session notes or client details is the high-risk step; a BAA-capable stack would be needed (cost and liability), so keep any AI feature to non-PHI tasks (policy drafting, checklists).
- State-specific licensing rules (home care, med spa, ABA) are the accuracy edge but also a liability: label state and date, sell annual updates.

## Evidence by audience

### 1. Home-care / caregiver agencies (score 28)
- Size 3: no headcount found in search results (the search said so explicitly). Agencies are numerous but far fewer than clinicians [estimate]. Needs a NAHC/state-licensing count lookup before relying on it.
- Money 5: policy-and-procedure manuals sell at $149 (universal) and $399 (state-reviewed); freelancers on Fiverr quote $50-$400 to write one [snippet] https://homecareatlas.com/for-agencies/policies-and-procedures , https://fiverr.com/remtee01/create-a-top-notch-policies-and-procedures-manual (neither opened). That is 25-60x a teacher sale. Owners buy with business money.
- Pain 5: state licensure surveys, Medicaid/payer audits, caregiver HR files, abuse/neglect and EVV rules; a missing policy fails an inspection. NY DOH publishes policy-writing guidance, showing the regulatory demand exists [snippet] https://Health.NY.Gov/facilities/home_care/docs/guidance_writing_policy_procedure.pdf .
- Recurrence 4: annual updates, state rule changes, new-hire packets and shift/visit logs repeat [estimate].
- Reach 3: Facebook groups and agency-startup consultants exist [estimate, not searched]; fewer public search terms than therapy; "start a home care agency" is a known search intent [estimate].
- Low AI 4: ChatGPT can draft generic policies, but state-licensing accuracy and inspector-ready formatting are what buyers pay for [estimate].
- Gap 4: seen only homecareatlas (a vertical tool) and Fiverr/sample sellers at the high price; no large Etsy-style template crowd surfaced [snippet-based, weak].

### 2. ABA providers (score 26)
- Size 3: 85,587 BCBA, 260,174 RBT (1 Jul 2026) [primary via report 08, BACB]. Buyers are BCBA practice owners and clinical directors, a few tens of thousands [estimate].
- Money 4: payer-funded (Medicaid/insurance) practices; $250/yr memberships and $5.50-$11.99 TPT data sheets seen in report 08/10 [snippet via earlier reports]. Business buyers can pay $30-$150 for a startup/audit kit [estimate].
- Pain 5: payer audits, BACB ethics code, authorization/billing documentation; denied claims cost real money [estimate].
- Recurrence 4: new-client onboarding, quarterly treatment-plan reviews, annual audits, 82,681 new RBTs in 2025 means constant training churn [primary, BACB via report 07].
- Reach 4: BCBA Facebook groups, ABA Instagram/TikTok communities, ABAI/CalABA conferences [estimate].
- Low AI 3: data-collection and billing-compliance packs are partially replaceable, and CentralReach/Catalyst software bundles forms [estimate, competitors not searched].
- Gap 3: report 08 saw crowded data-sheet space on TPT; practice-operations/audit kits less so [estimate].
- PHI: session data sheets, once filled, are PHI; sell blank forms only.

### 3. Dental / chiro / med-spa owners, non-clinical admin (score 26)
- Size 4: 202,536 dentists and 161,817 active dental practices (Apr 2026); 70,210 chiropractors across ~38,000 locations [snippet, third-party aggregator] https://withorbital.com/data/how-many-dentists-in-the-us/ , https://withorbital.com/data/how-many-chiropractors-in-the-us/ ; 10,488 med spas [snippet, AmSpa] https://americanmedspa.org/med-spa-statistics/ . Many dental practices are DSO-owned, so independent owners are fewer [estimate].
- Money 5: practice owners with real revenue; med spas especially. HIPAA kit seen at $129 [snippet] https://clinicdefender.gumroad.com/l/tecri (not opened).
- Pain 4: OSHA/HIPAA annual training, consent forms, state med-spa medical-director and scope rules.
- Recurrence 4: annual HIPAA/OSHA refreshers, onboarding checklists.
- Reach 3: Dentistry/med-spa Facebook groups, AmSpa, Dental Economics; heavily courted by consultants [estimate].
- Low AI 3, Gap 3: [estimate; not enough competitor evidence].
- Best sub-slice: med-spa admin (consents, treatment-record forms, state-compliance checklists), because the industry grows >25%/yr since 2020 [snippet, AmSpa] so new owners keep arriving.

## Best 2
1. **Home-care agency owners (28/35).** Highest price tolerance ($149-$399 seen), high-stakes licensing, annual refresh cycle, and AI cannot guarantee state accuracy. Weakness: unverified headcount and smaller audience; validate with a state-by-state pilot (start with 1-2 states).
2. **ABA practice owners/clinical directors (26/35; med-spa/dental admin is a tie at 26 and the runner-up).** Large certified base, payer-audit pain, constant new-RBT onboarding churn, reachable communities. Chosen over med-spa because report 08 already has ABA groundwork; pick med-spa instead if direct competitor checks show ABA data sheets are too crowded.

## Product ideas (price ranges)
