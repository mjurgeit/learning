## 4. Compliance and platform risks

1. **HIPAA / PHI.** Templates sold must be blank. Never publish mockups, screenshots or sample notes using real client names, initials plus dates, DOBs, or real session text. Use obviously fictional sample data. PHI includes therapy notes, recordings, appointment data and any identifier linked to a communication disorder or treatment [snippet, accountablehq.com - a compliance-vendor blog, not law]. The seller is not a covered entity for blank forms, but a buyer who stores PHI in a Google Sheet or Notion workspace is the one with exposure. Add a plain-language disclaimer: the product is a blank template, not a HIPAA-compliant system, users must follow their employer's/payer's policies. Not legal advice; consider a one-time lawyer review of the disclaimer text.
2. **Do not copy standardized assessments.** Test materials (record forms, items, scoring) are protected by copyright and trade secret; publishers such as Pearson advise against sharing test material [snippet, kspope.com page on Pearson/HIPAA]. Do not reproduce item content, score conversion tables or record forms from CELF, GFTA, PLS, Vineland, etc. You may build an *original* generic tracker that has a field "Test name / raw score / standard score". Avoid using test names in listing titles as a keyword-stuffing play (trademark, plus Etsy IP takedown risk). Descriptive/nominative use ("works alongside your assessment") only, with no logos.
3. **Trademarks and credentials.** "RBT", "BCBA", "BACB" are BACB certification marks; ASHA and AOTA logos and "ASHA-approved/endorsed" claims are off-limits. Say "for RBTs and BCBAs" descriptively, never "BACB-approved". Do not claim a form satisfies payer, Medicaid, state or school-district documentation requirements; payer rules vary.
4. **Clinical-claims risk.** Goal banks must be original wording; do not copy goal text from commercial banks or from state standards documents with restrictive terms. Do not describe a template as diagnostic or as replacing clinical judgment.
5. **Platform rules.** Etsy requires AI disclosure for AI-assisted designs (see 01-market-report). TPT forbids copying and requires originality; TPT's Basic payout is 55% (+$0.30 per item), Premium 80% for $59.95/yr [snippet, third-party fee guides; confirm in seller dashboard].
6. **Scope-of-practice and consent in parent-facing products.** Avoid anything that looks like a signed consent or treatment plan form with legal effect; label as "template - adapt to your practice's requirements".

## 6. Where a new entrant can differentiate

1. **Narrow, role-specific systems instead of generic sheets.** "School SLP caseload system: IEP goal tracker + group data sheets + quarterly progress-note generator" beats a pile of 5 sheets for $4.92. Same for "OT school-based", "RBT session note + parent summary".
2. **Software-like formats.** Google Sheets/Forms with auto-calculated percent-correct and cue-level breakdown; fillable/editable PDFs; Notion. Snippets show most competing items are static printable/editable PDFs.
3. **Auto-generated progress-report language.** Sheets that convert tracked data into a draft progress-report sentence. Original wording only.
4. **Bundles by setting** (school, early intervention/home health, private practice, telehealth). EI/home-health therapists were visible as under-served in snippets (the SOAP "early intervention" listing at $8.99 is one of few).
5. **Trust signals competitors lack:** a "no PHI, offline-capable, no accounts" statement; a clear license (single user); free sample sheet as lead magnet.
6. **Do not compete on price.** The low end ($1-$5) is a race to the bottom; sell at $9-$29 and bundles at $29-$49.

## 7. Verdict

**Conditionally justified as a small side line, not as a standalone business.**

For:
- Large, well-paid, growing audience (BLS +14-15% growth; ~700k certified/licensed people) [primary/estimate].
- Proven product-market fit for the basic product: 100+ reviews on single TPT data-sheet items at $5.50-$12 [snippet]; the leading SLP brands run $200-$250/yr subscriptions [snippet], so the audience pays for convenience.
- Dense Etsy supply for ABA and SOAP templates shows search demand [snippet].

Against:
- No visible bestseller badges or large sales counts in any Etsy therapist snippet; the only Etsy sales count seen was 12 in ~10 months [snippet]. Etsy price points are low ($3-$15).
- Incumbents (SLP Now, Speechy Musings, Speech Therapy Store, Mia McDaniel) own SLP search on TPT and in the community; trust matters in a profession that worries about documentation.
- Compliance overhead (PHI, test-copyright, trademarks) restricts mockups and keyword use.
- Unit economics need roughly 70 orders/month for $500 [estimate]; unproven.

Decision rule: proceed only with a narrow system (section 6), not generic sheets, and only if the 30-day test below clears its threshold.

## 8. 30-day validation test (cheap)

Cost: ~$20 listing fees (about 14 listings x $0.20 = $2.80) plus a free Payhip account, a free Pinterest account and a few hours of building. Under $30.

Build 2 original products with fictional sample data only:
- A: "School SLP Caseload and IEP Goal Tracker" (Google Sheets, auto percent-correct, progress-sentence drafter), $14.
- B: "RBT/BCBA Session Note + Data Sheet Bundle" (editable PDF + Docs), $9.

Week 1: publish A and B on Etsy (6-7 listings each via variants/angles: school SLP, EI SLP, OT, RBT, parent-summary; 13 tags each); put A and B on Payhip too, with a free one-page sample data sheet as an email lead magnet. Week 2-4: 3 Pinterest pins per week per product; post the free sample in 2-3 relevant public SLP/OT/ABA communities **only where self-promotion is permitted**; log Etsy views, favorites, orders, and Payhip downloads.

Success threshold at day 30 (all must hold, otherwise stop or pivot):
- Etsy listing views >= 400 total with conversion >= 1% (>= 4 orders), OR
- >= 10 total paid orders across Etsy + Payhip, AND
- >= 25 free-sample email signups, AND
- zero IP/compliance takedowns or complaints.

Kill: < 5 paid orders and < 10 signups at day 30. Pivot: if signups >= 25 but orders < 5, the audience wants the content but not at this price/format; test a $29 bundle and a $9 monthly membership waitlist. If >= 10 orders, expand to 20 listings and test one OT and one early-intervention bundle before committing to a catalog. (Threshold values are my judgment, not benchmarks from data.)
